## Design Specificity Verdict

**Split verdict: the copy layer is genuinely authored for VCA North Atlantic; the human/imagery layer is generic placeholder that hasn't caught up.**

**LLM assessment:** The written content isn't category-interchangeable filler — production-salary-with-no-negative-accrual pay, a named 6-month 1:1 mentorship structure with a 60–90 day no-solo-shift guarantee, a Medical Director track gated at "6+ years," free License Defense with an assigned attorney, a $3,500/year CE budget, a 3.5% 401(k) match vested day one, and a specific six-state GP footprint vs. six-state ER/specialty footprint. No generic vet-chain template ships FAQ answers this concrete, and the CSS custom properties are VCA's actual brand palette, not a default. Where it breaks down: Employee Spotlights features a placeholder-named "Alex Doe" with stock Unsplash photography, Team Testimonials uses "Dr. Smith / Tech Jane / Mark Johnson," and the Culture carousel is openly icon-tile placeholders (documented in a code comment) standing in for real photography — in contrast to the real-named recruiter cards elsewhere on the same page. A candidate gets specific, credible *claims*, illustrated by *generic* people and photography.

**Deterministic scan:** The CLI detector (`detect.mjs`) returned 11 findings across 6 rules on the static file; the in-browser detector (live DOM inspection) found 78 across 8 rules — the gap is expected, since the browser pass catches computed/rendered instances (pseudo-elements, `nth-child` variants) the static regex scan can't fully resolve. Rules that fired: `side-tab` (thick colored left/top border on a card — a recognized "AI-generated UI" tell, 3 static / 9 live hits across `.hospital-type`, `.diff-tile`, `.spotlight-interview-item`, and a `.card::before` stripe), `border-accent-on-rounded` (1 static / 6 live — the same accent-border pattern combined with `border-radius`), `overused-font` (Inter at ~65% of text — expected for a single-typeface brand system, low concern), `layout-transition` (animating `width`/`max-height`/`margin-top` instead of `transform` — 2 static / 4 live, minor jank risk), `low-contrast` (10 hits, see below), `ai-color-palette` (35 live hits), `image-hover-transform` (1 live), and `em-dash-overuse` (22, advisory).

Two categories of detector findings are very likely **false positives**, and both assessments independently converged on this:
- **`broken-image` (index.html:1704):** The detector matched the literal text `<img>` inside a JS *comment* describing the carousel's fallback logic, not an actual empty `<img>` tag. The real code only ever renders a genuine `<img src="...">` when a slide supplies one; otherwise it renders a `<div class="placeholder-tile">`. No broken image ships.
- **6 of the 10 `low-contrast` hits ("white on #f7f7f7", 1.1:1):** These are white text sitting on a hero gradient or a semi-transparent navy overlay over a photo (`rgba(0, 59, 113, 0.75)`), not on the page's flat `#f7f7f7` background. The detector's ancestor-walk can't resolve a `linear-gradient()` or `rgba()` overlay background, so it falls through to `<body>`'s color and reports a false 1.1:1. Screenshots confirm the real contrast is high.
- **`ai-color-palette` (35 hits, purple headings + teal accents):** This rule is designed to catch generic, unconsidered AI-default color choices — but in this case the flagged colors *are* VCA's actual brand palette (`--blue:#003B71`, `--teal:#00A99D`, `--heading-purple:#7D3F98`, pulled from the real logo), which the codebase documents deliberately in a CSS comment explaining which tints are decorative-only vs. WCAG-AA-safe for text. The detector can't know brand intent from color values alone — this reads as a false positive specific to this project, not evidence of generic styling.

The remaining 4 of 10 `low-contrast` hits are real and worth fixing: `#777777` secondary text on `#f7f7f7` (4.2:1, needs 4.5:1 — a near-miss on `.testimonial-role`) and `#00A99D` teal text on `#f7f7f7` (2.7:1, needs 3:1). The `side-tab`/`border-accent-on-rounded` cluster is also real — both assessments flagged the same underlying pattern independently (the LLM review called the border-color `nth-child` cycling "arbitrary rather than meaningful" under Heuristic 4; the detector counted 9–15 concrete instances of it).

**Visual overlays:** Browser injection succeeded — the live detector drew visible yellow-outlined overlay boxes directly on the rendered page (captured in both a 1400px desktop and 375px mobile screenshot), confirming the flagged elements are real, visible, on-screen nodes rather than stale/off-screen matches.

## Overall Impression

The content strategy is doing real work — the FAQ in particular reads like someone sat down with actual candidate objections and answered them, and the accessibility engineering (documented WCAG-safe color variants, real focus traps, `prefers-reduced-motion` support) is well above what a template microsite usually gets. The gap between that level of craft and the page's remaining placeholder content (fake phone numbers, identical "Apply Now" destinations, stock-photo team members) is the single biggest issue — it's not that the design is generic, it's that specific parts of it are visibly unfinished sitting right next to parts that are clearly finished, and the unfinished parts cluster at the two highest-trust moments on the page: the human-connection sections (Testimonials/Spotlights) and the final Contact section right before the footer.

**Important context before you read the Priority Issues below:** several of these were already known, already-discussed limitations from earlier in this build — not new discoveries. The Culture carousel's placeholder tiles were an explicit decision made together ("use stock for now") because real photography isn't available yet. The generic Apply link was explicitly flagged as a stand-in, with the `data-apply-role` config built specifically to make wiring in real per-role links easy once your backend team finishes that work. I'm keeping them in the report because the skill's job is to score what's actually shipping today, but they're not surprises.

## What's Working

1. **FAQ depth and plain language.** Answers like explaining "a slower quarter never means paying anything back" (de-jargoning negative accrual) do real persuasive work by preemptively resolving the exact anxieties a doctor evaluating an offer would have.
2. **Deliberate accessibility engineering.** A CSS comment explicitly documents why the tint colors are decorative-only while the `-dark` variants are the WCAG-AA-safe text colors; there's a real skip-link, `:focus-visible` outlines, a `prefers-reduced-motion` kill-switch, and a correctly-implemented modal focus trap with scroll-position preservation. The detector pass backs this up — no critical accessibility-blocking findings, only two borderline contrast near-misses.
3. **Concrete, credible numbers over marketing platitudes.** "6 weeks of PTO for GP doctors, 5 for ER doctors," "$3,500/year CE budget," "3.5% match, 100% vested day one" — specificity that reads as researched and true.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Fake, sequential phone numbers under real recruiter names**
- **What:** `(123) 456-7890`, `-7891`, `-7892` sit beneath three real, named recruiters (Nick Silva, Amy Trudeau, Lauren Gerrish), flagged only by an HTML comment invisible to any visitor.
- **Why it matters:** This is the page's final content section before the footer — the last thing a candidate sees right before deciding to reach out — and per the peak-end rule, that's the worst possible place to leave the most visibly fake element on the page.
- **Fix:** Swap in real direct lines, or drop the `tel:` links and rely on the (already-real) `mailto:` links until numbers exist.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`
- **Known already?** Yes — flagged with a `TODO` comment in an earlier session. Worth confirming whether real numbers exist yet.

**[P1] "Apply Now" is role-differentiated in copy but identical in destination**
- **What:** Every role card and "View All Open Positions" route to the same `https://careers.vca.com`, explicitly marked as a placeholder in the code.
- **Why it matters:** The Open Roles section is presented as role-specific; a candidate who clicks under Veterinary Technicians and lands on a generic careers homepage with no technician filter reasonably concludes the site oversold what's available, right at the conversion moment.
- **Fix:** Wire real per-role/location requisition URLs into the already-built `APPLY_LINKS` config, or land on a pre-filtered search query at minimum.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`
- **Known already?** Yes — this was built deliberately as a placeholder architecture pending your backend team's work. Flagging so you can confirm whether that work has landed.

**[P1] Testimonials and Spotlights use placeholder identities**
- **What:** "Alex Doe" (a literal Doe placeholder) with stock photography; "Dr. Smith," "Tech Jane," "Mark Johnson" — none read as real, verifiable people, unlike the real-named recruiter cards elsewhere on the same page.
- **Why it matters:** Experienced doctors evaluating multiple offers — a core audience for this page — are the most likely to notice generic names/stock photos, and noticing it erodes trust in every other claim on the page.
- **Fix:** Replace with real, named team members and real photography when available, or reduce this section's visual prominence until it can carry real content.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit` (catalog remaining placeholders sitewide first), then `$impeccable delight` once real assets exist.
- **Known already?** Partially — this was a deliberate interim choice given no real photography was available, but the specific placeholder *names* (vs. photos) weren't previously flagged as their own issue.

**[P2] Repeated "accent border on a rounded card" pattern reads as templated**
- **What:** The detector found 9–15 instances (both static and live scans) of the same visual move — a 4px solid accent border combined with `border-radius` — across `.hospital-type`, `.diff-tile`, `.benefit-tile`, `.spotlight-interview-item`, and a `.card::before` stripe, with border colors cycling through brand colors via `nth-child` rather than carrying any actual meaning.
- **Why it matters:** This is a specific, named pattern recognized as a generic "AI-generated UI" tell — repeated this many times, it reads as templated rather than intentional, even though the individual colors are correct brand colors.
- **Fix:** Reduce to 1-2 places where the accent border actually signals something (e.g., only on the currently-relevant benefit tile), and drop it elsewhere in favor of the existing shadow/spacing system to carry separation.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`
- **Known already?** No — new finding from the detector pass.

**[P2] Two real (non-false-positive) contrast near-misses**
- **What:** `#777777` secondary text on `#F7F7F7` is 4.2:1 (needs 4.5:1) on `.testimonial-role`; `#00A99D` teal text on `#F7F7F7` is 2.7:1 (needs 3:1).
- **Why it matters:** Both are small/secondary text, so the practical impact is limited, but they're genuine WCAG AA near-misses, not detector noise — worth closing out given how much other accessibility work is already done correctly.
- **Fix:** Darken `#777` slightly or increase size; swap standalone teal text to `--teal-dark` per the codebase's own existing convention for text-on-light contexts.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable audit`
- **Known already?** No — new finding from the detector pass.

**[P2] Primary CTA disappears exactly when mobile users are most engaged**
- **What:** The sticky mobile Apply bar is hidden (`display:none!important`) while the hamburger nav or the Spotlight modal is open. Inside the open mobile menu, "Open Roles" is styled identically to "FAQ" — it loses the distinct CTA-button treatment it gets on desktop.
- **Why it matters:** These are the two moments a mobile candidate is most actively engaged (reading an interview, browsing sections) — exactly when a persistent Apply affordance matters most, and exactly when it's gone.
- **Fix:** Keep a minimal persistent Apply affordance even with the modal/menu open; give "Open Roles" distinct button styling inside the mobile nav list to match desktop.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`
- **Known already?** No — new finding.

**[P3] Two 6-item content grids exceed comfortable chunking**
- **What:** Benefits and Why VCA each present six co-equal tiles in one flat grid with no sub-category headers.
- **Why it matters:** A candidate scanning for one specific thing (e.g. parental leave) has to visually parse all six uniformly-weighted tiles instead of jumping to a labeled cluster.
- **Fix:** Group into 2-3 labeled clusters, or add a lightweight visual lead-in.
- **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`
- **Known already?** No — new finding.

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer — e.g. a new-grad DVM student):**
- The header presents 8 simultaneous top-level destinations before she's read anything, and has to guess whether the FAQ she needs is one of the 6 visible links or hidden inside "More" (it's hidden).
- The hero requires reading a full block quote from "Lauren McVey, Group Vice President" — an unfamiliar title with no context — before reaching the "See Open Roles" CTA.
- Clicking "Apply Now" under Veterinarians lands her on the generic careers homepage; as a first-timer she has no way to know this is a known placeholder and may assume North Atlantic hospitals aren't actually listed there.
- "GP doctors" / "ER doctors" is used without expansion — a CSR or vet-tech candidate may not immediately parse it.

**Riley (Deliberate Stress Tester):**
- All four footer social icons and three footer resource links ("New Grad Programs," "Internships," "Continuing Education") are dead `#` placeholders — for exactly the new-grad audience the page claims to serve equally, the three most relevant links are dead.
- Clicking "Apply Now" under all three role cards in sequence reveals they open the identical URL — the role-specific framing is cosmetic.
- The Spotlight modal has no URL/hash state, so there's no way to deep-link, bookmark, or share "Sam Lee's story" directly.
- Sequential recruiter phone numbers are trivially identified as fake on sight, not just on a call attempt.

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User):**
- On a narrow viewport, three sentences of executive block-quote sit between the H1 and the primary CTA — she has to scroll past prose one-handed before reaching a tappable action.
- Inside the mobile menu, "Open Roles" loses its distinct desktop button styling, so the primary action loses visual priority exactly where mobile users spend more time deciding.
- The sticky Apply bar vanishes during her two most active engagement states (menu open, modal open).
- The page depends on three external CDNs (fonts, all icons via unpkg, all photography via Unsplash) with no local fallback for any of them — on filtered hospital/corporate wifi or a flaky connection, any one failing silently blanks icons or leaves broken-image gaps sitewide with zero graceful degradation. (Both assessments independently hit this exact failure in the sandbox; worth treating as a real resilience gap, not just a sandbox artifact, given hospital wifi is a plausible real-world context for this audience.)

## Minor Observations

- Footer social icons use generic, mismatched glyphs (at-sign for Facebook, camera for Instagram, `link` for LinkedIn, message-circle for X) — even once real URLs exist, users can't visually identify the platform without reading the small `aria-label`.
- The Region at a Glance grid lists MI and OH only under "Emergency & Specialty" on a page branded "North Atlantic" — both are Midwest states; worth a sanity check that this is intentional and not stale data.
- Carousel autoplay advances every 3s indefinitely; `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected at the OS level, but there's no visible pause control for a sighted user who simply finds constant auto-advance distracting.
- The Lucide CDN `<script>` tag is unpinned (no version lock, no SRI hash, no local fallback) — a breaking upstream release or the CDN being unreachable silently blanks every icon sitewide.
- Several sections' entry state is `opacity:0` until an `IntersectionObserver` callback fires — if JS fails to execute for any reason, that content stays permanently invisible; no `<noscript>` fallback exists anywhere.
- `.nav-links a::after` and `.spotlight-quote` animate `width`/`max-height`/`margin-top` instead of `transform` (detector: `layout-transition`, 4 live hits) — low real-world jank risk here since the affected elements are small/absolutely-positioned, but cheap to swap to `transform`-based equivalents.
- 22 em-dashes in body text (detector: advisory only) — not wrong, just worth a skim if a very tight editorial voice is a goal.
